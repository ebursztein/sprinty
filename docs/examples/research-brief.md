# Prepare a research brief

Use Sprinty for a research project that needs more than one session: gathering sources, evaluating evidence, writing, and review. The result is a brief you can inspect, not a promise that a passing gate proves every claim true.

## Paste into your agent

```text
Use Sprinty to produce a research brief in this Git-backed project.

Question: [the question to investigate]
Audience and decision it supports: [describe them]
Constraints: [scope, date range, length, or source requirements]

Read the project's instructions and Sprinty skills. Call info with the canonical repository path, then resume suitable work or start a sprint in the project's gitignored data directory. Share the dashboard URL.

Break the work into framing, evidence gathering, synthesis, and review. Create tasks with explicit deliverables and dependencies before writing. Claim work before editing and record decisions and progress as you go.

Save a scope note, a source register, a claim/evidence matrix, and a final Markdown brief under research/. Capture direct source URLs, relevant dates, what each source supports, and unresolved contradictions. Prefer primary sources. Clearly distinguish observations, estimates, and inference; do not manufacture citations or quotations.

Before completing synthesis, check that the questions are covered and important claims have supporting sources. Use executable checks for things they can actually test, such as required files or link format. Record factual and editorial review separately as manual evidence, explaining what was reviewed and any uncertainty. Do not count a format check as fact-checking.

Keep the research artifacts attached to their tasks, commit finished deliverables, and record the appropriate evidence. At a session boundary, leave a note with the current findings, unresolved questions, and next step. Recover that state with info and sprint_resume when continuing.

Finish with the brief, source register, limitations, and remaining review items. Do not send the brief to anyone or publish it externally.
```

## What to look for

The next session can identify the research question, inspect the source register, and continue the unfinished work. The final brief points to its evidence; limitations remain visible. Checks of file presence, source quality, and editorial judgment are described honestly as different kinds of verification.
